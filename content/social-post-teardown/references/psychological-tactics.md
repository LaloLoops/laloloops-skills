# Psychological Tactics Taxonomy

A short, neutral reference for naming the persuasion and engagement tactics
commonly used in short-form social posts. Use these names in the
`social-post-teardown` output so analysis stays consistent across posts.

Quote the exact span in the post that triggers the tactic. Do not invent
tactics that are not actually present in the text.

## Attention And Stop-Scroll

| Tactic | Definition | Common trigger shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pattern interrupt | Opens with an unexpected statement that breaks the reader's scrolling rhythm. | A flat declarative sentence that contradicts a default assumption. |
| Curiosity gap | Promises a payoff without revealing it, so the reader keeps reading to close the loop. | "Most people get this wrong about X." |
| Specific number | Replaces a vague claim with a precise figure to feel credible and concrete. | "I cut my deploy time by 37%." |
| Negative hook | Frames the post around what not to do, or a failure, to bypass advice fatigue. | "Stop doing X." or "I wasted a year on Y." |
| Contrast pair | Sets up a then/now, before/after, or expected/actual to create tension. | "Everyone says X. Here is what actually works." |

## Identity And Belonging

| Tactic | Definition | Common trigger shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In-group signaling | Uses vocabulary, references, or jokes only the target reader understands. | Inside jargon, role names, or niche tools. |
| Enemy framing | Names a shared opponent (a behavior, a stereotype, a competitor type) the reader can rally against. | "X people will hate this." |
| Confession | Reveals a personal mistake or unpopular opinion to feel honest and earn trust. | "I have to admit, I was wrong about X." |
| Status nod | Positions the reader as smarter, more serious, or more advanced than the implied baseline. | "If you already know X, you will love this." |

## Credibility

| Tactic | Definition | Common trigger shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social proof | Cites people, numbers, or institutions that vouch for the claim. | "10,000 builders use this." |
| Authority borrow | Quotes or references a recognized figure to inherit their credibility. | "As <known person> said..." |
| Specificity over polish | Uses oddly precise details to feel like lived experience instead of marketing. | "It was 11:47pm on a Tuesday." |
| Receipts | Shows screenshots, transcripts, or numbers as proof. | "Here is the exact log." |

## Emotion And Stakes

| Tactic | Definition | Common trigger shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loss framing | Describes what the reader is losing by not changing behavior. | "Every day you wait, you lose X." |
| Aspiration framing | Paints the desired future state the reader wants to inhabit. | "Imagine shipping in an afternoon." |
| Vulnerability | Shares a soft, honest emotion to drop defenses and invite connection. | "I almost quit last month." |
| Stakes escalation | Raises the cost or importance of the topic as the post progresses. | "And then it cost us the contract." |

## Cognitive Shortcuts

| Tactic | Definition | Common trigger shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rule of three | Groups examples or beats in threes for memorability and rhythm. | Three bullets, three nouns, three steps. |
| Listicle frame | Promises a finite, scannable list as the structure. | "5 things I wish I knew." |
| Anchoring | Plants a reference number or comparison early to shape later judgments. | "Most consultants charge $500/hour." |
| Reframing | Renames a familiar concept so the reader sees it freshly. | "It is not a bug, it is a feature." |

## Engagement Mechanics

| Tactic | Definition | Common trigger shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open question | Ends with a direct question to invite replies. | "What would you add?" |
| Save bait | Promises a list, checklist, or framework worth bookmarking. | "Save this for later." |
| Share bait | Frames the post so sharing signals the reader's identity or taste. | "Tag a founder who needs this." |
| Comment trap | Includes a small inaccuracy or hot take that invites correction. | "There are only 3 ways..." (when readers will list a 4th). |
| Cliffhanger thread | Ends post 1 of N mid-thought so readers continue. | "I will explain in the next post." |

## Structural Devices

| Tactic | Definition | Common trigger shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-sentence paragraphs | Uses whitespace to slow the reader and emphasize each line. | One line, blank, one line. |
| Parallel construction | Repeats grammatical shape across lines for rhythm and memorability. | "You will X. You will Y. You will Z." |
| Callback | Repeats a phrase from the opening at the close to feel composed. | Same noun in line 1 and last line. |
| Em-dash pivot | Uses a dash to set up a sudden tonal shift mid-sentence. | "It worked - until it didn't." |

## How To Use This Taxonomy

- Prefer names from this list. If the post uses a tactic not listed here,
  describe it in plain language and flag it as "not in taxonomy" so the
  reference can grow over time.
- A single post rarely uses more than 5-7 of these. Resist the urge to label
  everything. List only the tactics that actually carry weight.
- Pair each tactic with the exact quoted span that triggers it. If you cannot
  quote it, you cannot claim it.
